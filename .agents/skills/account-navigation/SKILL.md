# Account Navigation & Multi-Threading

Cold outreach to a company rarely succeeds on a single contact. People change jobs, ignore inboxes, go on leave, or don't have authority. Account navigation is the practice of methodically working multiple contacts at one target account without looking like a spam operation.

---

## The Rule of Multi-Threading

**Never rely on one person at an account. Never email the whole company at once.**

- **Target account size 10-50**: Identify 1-2 people. Contact sequentially.
- **Target account size 51-200**: Identify 2-4 people across 2 relevant functions (e.g. Sales + Ops). Contact staggered.
- **Target account size 201-1000**: Identify 3-6 people. Run 2 parallel threads in different departments.
- **Target account size 1000+**: Treat business units as separate accounts.

## Entry Points: Top-Down vs Bottom-Up

### Top-Down (C-Suite / Founder / VP)
Reach high when:
- The problem is strategic, existential, or board-visible (e.g., missed revenue targets, replatforming)
- The purchase requires budget reallocation, not existing spend
- The company is under 100 people (founders still buy tools)

How:
- Ultra-short email (<60 words)
- Focus on business outcomes, not features
- Ask for referral if not the right person: "If this isn't your remit, who on the team owns [function]?"

### Bottom-Up (Manager / Lead / Practitioner)
Reach practitioners when:
- The product replaces a daily workflow tool they hate
- You need internal intelligence before approaching the decision-maker
- You have a free tier or self-serve trial they can test themselves

How:
- Focus on specific, painful day-to-day workflow friction
- Ask about how they currently solve the problem, not whether they'll buy
- Turn them into a champion: "If we can prove this saves 3 hours/week, would you introduce me to [VP Title]?"

### The Sandwich Approach (Recommended)
1. **Day 1**: Email practitioner/manager with a specific technical observation or question.
2. **Day 3**: Email VP/Director with a high-level outcome message.
3. If practitioner responds: Use their insight (anonymized) to re-frame the conversation with the VP: "Chatted with someone on your ops team who mentioned [friction]..."
4. If VP responds: Loop in the practitioner they delegate to.

## Staggering Rules (Avoid Internal Collision)

Nothing kills a deal faster than two executives turning to each other and saying "did you get spammed by this person too?"

1. **Minimum 5 business days** between contacting different people at the same company unless using an intentional multi-threaded campaign.
2. **Never send the exact same email** to two people at the same company. Different angles, different subject lines.
3. **If anyone replies**, pause all other active sequences at that company immediately until the conversation resolves.
4. **If anyone opts out** ("unsubscribe", "not interested"), evaluate if the opt-out is personal ("I am not the right person") or organizational ("our company does not buy this"). Organizational opt-out = suppress the entire company domain.

---

## Referral Mining (When You Reach the Wrong Person)

When someone says "not my department" or "talk to X":

1. **Respond within 2 hours** while the email is fresh.
2. **Thank them warmly** and ask for permission to use their name:
   > "Thanks, [Name] — really appreciate the pointer. Mind if I mention to [X] that you pointed me their way?"
3. **Open the referral email**:
   > "Subject: [Name] suggested I reach out\n\n[X], [Name] mentioned you're the right person to speak with regarding [function]..."
   A warm referral email gets 3-5x the reply rate of cold outreach.

## Contact State Tracking

For every company in Harvey's pipeline:
- `researched`: Account mapped, contacts identified
- `contacted`: Active sequence running on primary contact
- `multi_threaded`: Active sequences on 2+ contacts (staggered)
- `engaged`: At least one contact has replied positively
- `champion_identified`: Internal advocate actively helping navigate the account
- `suppressed`: Account-level opt-out, do not contact any employee
